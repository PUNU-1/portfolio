# Proposal templates (English)

A strong proposal is short and about the client, not about you. The structure never changes:

1. **Show you read the brief:** one sentence in your own words or a specific question about the task.
2. **One piece of proof:** a link to the closest case in your portfolio.
3. **Plan, deadline, price:** brief, no fluff.
4. **Next step:** one clear question or offer.

Don't paste a template as is. Replace `{{...}}` and always rewrite the first paragraph for the specific job: clients spot copy-pasted bids instantly. Send 3–5 careful bids a day rather than 30 identical ones.

---

## 1. Landing page or brochure site

> Hi {{name}},
>
> I read your brief: you need {{a landing page for ... / a brochure site ...}}, and the main goal is {{getting leads / showing services ...}}. Two quick questions: {{do you have copy and a logo ready? which sites do you like?}}
>
> A similar piece in my portfolio: {{link to case 3, 4 or 5}}: responsive build, light and dark themes, a lead form, basic SEO. It's an open-source demo project, so you can open it and check it on your phone.
>
> My proposal: {{1–2 days for the scope and a first-screen mockup, then the build}}. Timeline {{N}} days, price {{N}}. Payment in stages: {{part}} upfront, the rest on acceptance. {{Two}} revision rounds are included.
>
> If that works, send me the answers and I'll write a one-page scope within {{time}}.

## 2. Telegram bot (leads, FAQ, bookings)

> Hi {{name}},
>
> Did I understand correctly: the bot should {{take bookings / answer common questions / collect leads}} for {{business}}, and {{the owner}} should get notifications? A question: {{where will the bot run (your server, or I can help with hosting)? do you need a client database?}}
>
> Related work: {{case 1 (booking bot) or case 2 (AI assistant)}}: {{link}}. It's a demo project on a fictional company; the code is open, with automated tests and a run guide.
>
> Plan: {{one-page scope → first version with the core flow → testing → handover of code and guide}}. Timeline {{N}} days, price {{N}}. I fix bugs free of charge for {{two weeks}} after delivery.
>
> What are your budget and deadline? I'll shape the scope to fit.

## 3. AI assistant

> Hi {{name}},
>
> You want a bot that answers customers from a knowledge base. An important question: {{do you have an FAQ or documents ready? which model/provider suits you, and how will you pay for the API?}}
>
> My approach: the bot answers only from your materials and doesn't make things up (if there's no answer it hands over to an operator), it states honestly that it is an AI, and it limits messages per user so costs don't spiral. Example: {{link to case 2}}. Model API costs are paid separately.
>
> Timeline {{N}} days, price {{N}}. I can start with a short prototype on 10–15 questions from your FAQ so you can judge quality before we scale up.

## 4. Design only (mockup)

> Hi {{name}},
>
> To design {{the page/site}} I need to understand: {{the target audience, which sites you like and dislike, whether you have brand colors and fonts}}.
>
> An example of how I approach landing design: {{link to case 5}}. I worked out the structure, a single accent color, a feature grid and responsive versions.
>
> Proposal: {{page structure and your feedback → first-screen mockup → full mockup}}. Timeline {{N}} days, {{N}}, {{two}} revision rounds.

## 5. Shop or payment integration

> Hi {{name}},
>
> A question about payments: {{which payment provider do you use or plan to use? do you have a contract with them?}} I don't register payment accounts on a client's behalf: the contract and keys are yours.
>
> Example of my work: {{link to case 6}}: the payment layer is an adapter, the webhook verifies the signature, the amount and duplicate deliveries, and orders have created / paid / failed statuses. It's a demo with a test payment step; no real payments happen in it.
>
> Timeline {{N}} days, price {{N}}. I'll make sure to test in the provider's test mode before live payments are switched on.

## If the client goes quiet (once, after 2–3 days)

> Hi {{name}}, following up on your brief: if it's still relevant I'm ready to discuss details and start. If you picked another contractor, thanks for your time and good luck with the project.

## If the brief lacks information

> To give you an exact price I need to clarify three things: {{1}}, {{2}}, {{3}}. For now my ballpark is {{range}}. Once you answer I'll send a fixed price and deadline.

## Replying to "can you do it cheaper?"

> I can reduce the scope so the price becomes {{N}}: {{what we cut}}. I won't lower the price for the same scope, because quality would suffer. Which option works for you?

## What not to do

- Don't write "I'll do whatever you need" without understanding the brief.
- Don't accept a free "test task" (offer demo projects and milestone payments instead).
- Don't move communication or payment off the platform: you lose deal protection and the client gets a chance not to pay.
- Don't take jobs you aren't sure about: it is better to say honestly "I don't do that part."
